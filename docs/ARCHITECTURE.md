# Architecture

M7 local handoffs remain separate from the accepted city graph. Lower Canal's north-quay join carries plain position/direction/width and a 30 m connected-lease retention approach. `cityDemand` retains its already loaded ward through the turn, and `citySafetyGates` adds a guard on supported ground until the opposite destination activates collision. `cityBoundaryDistance` includes this guard approach for boundary-needed timing; exact crossing still records the authoritative macro footprint. The shared two-lease limit, staged preparation, cancellation and departure timers remain in force.

M1 established the runtime boundaries and renderer decision below. M2–M4 implement the rural/living slice and one authored district. M5 implements bounded three-area streaming; full-city generation remains a proposal.

## M7 controlled production districts

`?scene=m7` uses the merged M6.1 macro blueprint and adds exactly two production detail areas: Central Market and Lower Canal. `urban-contracts.ts` contains plain serializable recipes, lots, facade/roof parameters, props, local surfaces, routes, views, persistent NPC navigation and ambient-zone weights. `urban-grammar.ts` limits combinations to district-specific architectural families and weighted recipes. Named parcels and deliberate civic/guild compounds control composition; the weights do not populate the whole city. Accepted courses, fourteen district IDs, seventeen adjacency pairs, thirty-seven road records, twenty-one portals and the water graph remain authoritative. The belfry proxy is suppressed only in M7, where the authored tower occupies its existing position.

`urban-world.ts` adapts those records to closed front/rear entrance hooks and independent collision proxies. All rendered door approaches are tested. Five Lower Canal rear elevations use windows because the existing terrace provides no supported door apron. Small local coplanar road-shoulder joints seal measured support gaps without changing original macro surfaces. The timber cargo dock follows outside the existing bank face, with a shallow connected stair run to a 0.6 m landing above the unchanged -1.16 m water datum.

`render/urban.ts` composes the existing all-side `district.kit` GLB through the same incremental scheduler, cache and resource scopes as River Market. Contextual stone, service and timber pigment is generated locally by `urban-paving.ts`; no reference pixels or new asset exports are used. Curated garden pockets reuse the accepted botanical geometry and global wind/wetness hooks. Roof envelopes join the existing rain system. Mutable instances, NPC figures and material clones belong to district leases; immutable templates and maps belong to the bounded renderer cache.

M7 endpoint V8 graphs identify r186.1's module-private `_bindingGroupsCache` retaining disposed uniform-only `render` groups under long-lived renderer/context keys. `render/shared-render-bindings.ts` adapts only the active streaming backend's newly created builders after initialization. It uses the installed ordinary BindGroup factory with local nonshared metadata, then attaches the real shared uniforms; group nodes, shader generation and global prototypes remain unchanged. Identical cached NodeBuilderState instances continue sharing their bindings; sharing across different builder states is deliberately removed so normal final-reference disposal can release them. Other group paths delegate unchanged. This pinned-version compatibility decision has real-NodeBuilder tests, initialized backend checks and separate measured lifecycle/timing verification. Reassess it on a Three upgrade; upstream now manages the shared cache in Bindings with final-reference eviction (see SOURCES).

The resident population grows from 42 to 94 identities: 32 merchants, customers, civic workers and travelers in Central Market, and 20 residents, porters and waterside craftspeople in Lower Canal. `NpcNavigation` combines their authored graphs with the accepted definitions. Existing JSON state, residency tiers, rain/night targets and interaction visits persist independently of render objects. One audio context and its existing five loops reuse a pair of localized emitters, with different market/workshop weights and the authoritative river graph. Time, weather, wind, input and settings remain shared.

`diagnostics/urban-repetition.ts` reports concrete identical facade, silhouette and shopfront signatures, plus neighboring matching roof combinations. It supplies building IDs for human camera review. The panel and full report are development-only; there is no global variety score. Human art/feel acceptance gates further expansion. Current actual checks, captures, measured readiness/frame tails and lifecycle limits are recorded in STATUS and reference/REVIEWS.

## Stack and boundaries
TypeScript + Vite + vanilla Three.js, DOM HUD/settings, and Rapier character physics are implemented in M1. Exact dependency versions are pinned in package.json with a real package-lock.json; use npm ci. Blender GLB remains the asset pipeline. No React is needed for this milestone.

`src/simulation/` owns serializable world, clock, schedules and interaction state. `src/render/` adapts that state to Three.js scene/cameras/materials. `src/physics/` bridges Rapier and simulation. `src/input/` maps actions. `src/ui/` owns DOM overlays. `src/assets/` owns manifest/loading/reference counting. `src/diagnostics/` owns capture and timing. Create modules only as needed, not empty engine hierarchies.

M1 uses a 60 Hz fixed simulation step with interpolated render positions. Catch-up is capped at six steps and pause/blur clears the accumulator. The course is authored deterministic data with stable IDs and seed 104729; the seed identifies this authored fixture and does not randomize its layout. State is plain serializable data, with no Three.js or Rapier objects.

M2 adds `simulation/rural-layout.ts` as the shared meter/palette contract and `simulation/rural.ts` as the authored environment. Optional triangle surfaces in `CourseSpec` drive both visible terrain and static Rapier collision; separate cuboid proxies represent architecture, bridge and significant obstacles. Each course supplies its own recovery bounds. The M1 course and mechanics remain selectable with `?scene=m1`; the rural slice remains selectable with `?scene=m2`. M5 is now the default; the resident M4 remains at `?scene=m4`; `?stage=blockout` exposes structural massing using the same terrain/collision.

`render/rural.ts` owns deterministic environment geometry, vegetation instances and water presentation. `assets/rural.ts` loads the Blender hero GLBs, verifies meter bounds and reports imported material/triangle facts before placement. `render/landscape-materials.ts` creates project-authored pigment textures; it never reads the reference PNGs. Existing renderer initialization, warmup, fallback diagnostics, capture harness and disposal own the new resources. Instance buffers are explicitly disposed as well as shared geometry/material/texture resources.

M2.1 splits botanical geometry and clustered communities into `render/rural-ecology.ts`, with reusable geometry and placement contracts in `render/rural-geometry.ts`. `render/hero-materials.ts` adds generated pigment maps, local UV coordinates and ground-contact colors to the existing GLBs without modifying their position/index data or files. Terrain render triangles receive coplanar subdivision for contextual pigment; the serialized Rapier surfaces remain unchanged. Decorative stone treads replace visible stair cuboids, and a bank gradient replaces visible shore strips; original stair and shoreline collision remains authoritative. These are art treatments over the accepted M2 slice, not new simulation systems.

`src/main.ts` owns startup, DOM settings, clock integration and disposal. `src/cameras/` owns gameplay/debug camera transforms. Physics owns one kinematic capsule for both gameplay views. A development-only `?test=1` harness exposes fixed-step actions and bookmarks for reproducible tests; it is not exposed by the production build. See TESTING.md for evidence and lifecycle coverage.

## Renderer decision gate
Try WebGPURenderer from `three/webgpu`; await initialization and record its actual backend. Its WebGL 2 fallback is not the same as promising every WebGPU feature runs on WebGL. Feature-gate compute-heavy effects, verify fallback and test material compatibility. Start with simple lit materials, sensible color management, directional shadows and fill light. TSL for needed custom shaders; no blanket demand to implement every advanced effect.

Do not mix legacy EffectComposer/ShaderMaterial/onBeforeCompile recipes into WebGPURenderer as though they were interchangeable. See SOURCES.md. If the installed version or GPU fails, record the cause and make one deliberate fallback decision; do not silently label a WebGL result WebGPU.

## Assets, ownership and scale
Explicit manifest IDs; pivots/units/LODs/collision metadata. Reuse resources, instance suitable repeated geometry and dispose by ownership/reference counts. Begin CPU-managed instancing; add GPU culling only after profiling. No one-Object3D-per-blade design.

## M5 bounded streaming ownership

`simulation/streaming-world.ts` partitions exactly three lifecycle areas: accepted rural, the existing River Market additions, and an eastern shell with three plain workshop masses. It filters rural data out of the market lease. Two narrow handoff floors remain resident; player, camera, authoritative environment and persistent state also remain resident. The neighboring shell is a testing assumption without new locals, interiors or gameplay. M5 is the default; `?scene=m4` preserves the full resident comparison, `?scene=m2` the rural slice and `?scene=m1` the course.

`streaming-contracts.ts` defines 36 m preload, 44 m deactivation and 52 m unload radii, with a 1.5 s outside delay. `streaming.ts` owns load/activate/deactivate/unload, one transport per area, abort plus epoch checks, obsolete-result disposal, bounded events and errors. Failed loads retry only after a true leave/reentry. The main adapter installs colliders before showing geometry. Inactive geometry is detached and its colliders removed; prepared resources remain until unload. Temporary destination gates clear only after active collision is installed. Normal movement uses the same capsule and no loading screen.

### M5.1 incremental preparation

The lifecycle now exposes `requested → preparing → warming → ready → active`, retaining inactive/unloaded/failed states, abort epochs and obsolete-result release. `main.ts` supplies a 10 s velocity lookahead for expensive areas and admits at most two instance/transport leases. The 36 m entry and 44 m deactivation radii and 1.5 s departure delay remain. The measured runtime retires inactive leases at 46 m instead of the controller's historical 52 m default: freeing the outgoing workshop slot 6 m earlier adds about 1.1 s at the 5.4 m/s run speed, covering the observed roughly 0.6 s rural readiness miss. Continuous departure and predictive return demand still govern retirement. The lightweight workshop uses ordinary distance preload. Reversal removes predictive demand; one transport per area remains enforced even if cancellation arrives during an unabortable native/parse wait.

Cold market preparation exceeds the direct run from natural spawn. Startup therefore prepares that single known neighbor before enabling Explore, while leaving the workshop unloaded. A stationary startup hint retains that ready neighbor; the first horizontal movement releases the hint to the normal predictive cancellation/departure rules. This deliberately trades startup waiting for a ready first handoff. It is not a loading screen during movement.

`render/preparation-scheduler.ts` executes authored construction generators in measured 3 ms slices, with a 64-job limit per slice and cancellable requestAnimationFrame yields. Individual jobs can exceed the target; their true duration, largest job, CPU sum, oversize count and yield count are reported. Pigment strokes, terrain subdivision/coloring, crowns/ecology, architecture/paving, instance transforms and NPC body/limb construction preserve their original equations and random sequence. Resident M2/M4 APIs exhaust the same generators synchronously.

`render/preparation-cache.ts` separates the renderer's immutable asset lifetime from each area instance. It admits at most 192 keys: eight generated maps, botanical geometry, four enriched GLB templates and fixed-area terrain. Area transforms, instance buffers, material clones and NPC figures remain independently owned. Reference accounting reports unique identities, cache references and instance references; canceled partial areas release their leases. Renderer teardown releases the cache. No reference image, GLB, accepted placement or content count was replaced.

Renderer warmup first compiles against the authoritative scene, then stages actual main/shadow submissions into a small offscreen target matching the live linear HDR color/depth/sample configuration. Every InstancedMesh receives a warmup job because its instance matrix/color buffer count affects generated WGSL. Only conservative static mesh equivalents are deduplicated. Two submissions share each queue wait; the measured scheduler still yields when its slice budget is reached. Each uses a distinct warmup camera so Three.js's same-camera/frame shadow suppression cannot skip the second pass. Temporary visibility, scene attachment and target state are restored synchronously before queue waiting or the next frame. Changed frustum flags stay confined to the detached destination and restore in the enclosing finally before readiness or cleanup. Existing compileAsync and queue-completion promises are not abortable: cancellation prevents further jobs, but final resource release waits for settlement. Queue waits are browser wall time, not GPU timestamps. Static deduplication is scoped to the current authored meshes; future per-object shader callbacks, batched meshes or differing geometry groups need additional signature fields or an explicit bypass.

Physics creates disabled destination proxies in measured jobs, keeping simulation and Rapier on the main thread. Activation enables proxies and populates broad phase before scene attachment and safety-gate release. Deactivation disables proxies; final unload removes them. `physics.areas` lists enabled area groups; total collider counts include disabled prepared proxies. The resident handoff floors and unavailable-destination guards remain authoritative.

`diagnostics/transition.ts` retains bounded chronological request reports independently of noisy frame spans: asset lookup/load, construction jobs, resource creation/reuse, environment hooks, compile/shadow submission/queue waiting, collider preparation/activation, NPC tier changes, audio, readiness, boundary need/crossing and first main-pass visible submission. Small jobs coalesce per yield slice without losing individual maxima. The development capture driver exports finished reports before releasing them for endpoint heap sampling; unfinished requests and late spans remain. Physical presentation, GPU execution and VRAM are unmeasured.

No worker was added: the demonstrated construction work is Three.js/canvas/resource adaptation, and the resulting pure placement jobs have not established a meaningful worker benefit. No off-thread rendering, WebAudio or Rapier operations are claimed. M5.1 measurements and gate status belong in STATUS.md; historical M5 evidence is preserved in `history/M5_STATUS.md`.

### M6 / M6.1 authored city topology

`simulation/city-contracts.ts` and `city-blueprint.ts` define fourteen stable districts and seventeen unique adjacency edges, polygon footprints, twelve terrain levels, a continuous five-reach water graph, thirty-seven road records, twenty-one gated connections, thirty landmark/structural bodies and bounded representative block massing. M6.1 introduces seven serializable `CityTerrace` records with district ownership, elevation, footprint, color and a named access road. Four secondary portals reuse existing adjacencies. `city-world.ts` anchors the accepted three courses verbatim. The city remains selected with `?scene=m6`; the default M5.1 corridor remains the comparable baseline. This is layout convergence, without a production generator or new gameplay system.

The cheap terrain skeleton, roads, bridge handoffs/rails and landmark proxies are resident. Eighteen grouped terrain trimeshes preserve cutouts: eleven district tops and seven intermediate terraces clipped to their owner's footprint. Renderer and Rapier consume the same `cityRetainingSurface`; its sides stop at neighboring street/terrace support and omit coplanar seams. Inclined roads have short level landings at joins; rendered/collision triangles share source data, with actual grades tested at or below 1:5. Visible stair treads sit over smooth controller support. Water remains at the accepted source datum; narrowed urban reaches alter geometry without changing endpoint topology. New district leases contain only representative masses, with no new GLBs or locals. `render/city-blueprint.ts` batches the macro presentation and supplies overview-only masses, capped landmarks, structural wall/gate/buttress volumes, clipped bridges and development layers. `city-navigation.ts` shares seven exact crossing spans between visual structures and resident rail collision.

City demand selects the nearest district and a connected velocity-directed neighbor. A 2 m portal clearance band temporarily retains the outgoing neighbor. The same two-instance/transport bound, preparation scheduler, abort epochs, ownership, native warmup and collision-before-visibility activation apply. At close forks, a city district excluded from current route demand deactivates and retires after a continuous 1.5 s graph departure; reversal resets the timer. M5 retains its measured 36/44/46 m runtime spatial policy unchanged. Portal guards are cached at the destination footprint inside the activation band, preventing both missing-detail entry and a ready-destination deadlock at the middle of a long bridge. Resident ground supports waiting. Graph departure changes city demand arbitration; it is not evidence that M5.1 radii alone suffice for city forks.

The deterministic master and seven debug cameras use the same world coordinate system. Third/first-person retain the original player/controller, with the city camera far range extended to 2200 m and a 2 m near plane only in eagle-eye. Extra overlay controls are development-only. Full overview proxy visibility does not activate all detail leases or expand the forty-two accepted NPC identities. The proxy presentation has its own explicit teardown; detailed leases keep the proven ownership path.

The following production-city infrastructure remains a proposal:

Author a district graph: terrain levels, waterways, paths, bridge connections, lots, navigation and hero landmarks before filling lots. Choose future chunk granularity from measurements; M5 uses one chunk per bounded area. Preserve its cancellation, residency and safe collision-handoff guarantees when scaling.

A single 2D heightfield is insufficient for stacked bridge surfaces and interiors. Treat those as separate geometry/collider/navigation layers. Navmesh generation is a later measured choice, not an unexplained dependency.

## Simulation expansion
M3 implements `simulation/environment.ts` as the authoritative serializable clear/cloudy/rain and day/dusk/night state. Four-second lighting/weather blends, wind, cloud coverage, rain, wetness and animation time advance only at fixed simulation ticks. Pausing freezes these values. Time presets are bounded authored states, not an accelerated astronomical clock. `render/environment.ts` applies the state to existing directional/fill lights, TSL vegetation displacement, surface wetness, conservative roof-clipped rain and a small distant cloud batch. It preserves source geometry, instance transforms, textures and collision; disposal restores original materials before normal scene cleanup. The existing water surface responds to wind/rain. Reduced mode lowers rain/cloud counts, uses one wind harmonic and updates water at 15 Hz.

`simulation/npcs.ts` owns six named locals, authored dialogue and a deterministic waypoint graph confined to the accepted cottage terrace. Day/dusk routes idle and walk; rain/night sends them via connected graph edges to existing cottage eaves. Paths are tested against actual Rapier capsule overlaps and ground rays. Locals have no dynamic collider or crowd avoidance; they remain nonblocking. `render/npcs.ts` owns solid all-side figures and limb poses: nearby full updates, distant 4 Hz, reduced nearby 8 Hz. `simulation/interaction.ts` selects nearby NPCs or two existing landmarks by three-dimensional reach; F opens/closes local text and pauses the addressed NPC's route. Leaving reach or pausing closes the panel.

`audio/audio.ts` creates Web Audio only from Explore/Resume's click. Deterministic filtered noise supports wind/river/rain; spatial PannerNodes follow the player/listener, footsteps distinguish authored surface regions, and short tonal cues accompany nearby locals/interactions. Master/ambience/footsteps/local controls and pause mute use gain ramps. Three ambient loops plus at most five transient voices are bounded; all nodes/sources/context are explicitly released. Development-only internal recording supplies audio evidence without a microphone. Sound is procedural, with no recorded speech. M3 does not need a persistent save UI: state serialization/continuation is tested, and each reload deliberately starts the same clear/day slice and settings.

One environment state drives time, lighting, wind, cloud/rain, material wetness and audio. NPC tiers: nearby full animation/navigation; local reduced update; unloaded data-only schedule. Stable identities avoid teleport/reset bugs. Weather-aware behavior begins with simple authored shelter destinations, not full live cognition.

Sound starts after a user gesture; attenuation, voice limits and volume categories. Saves are versioned local state (IndexedDB when warranted), not secrets or remote accounts. No server/model API required for the first playable world.
